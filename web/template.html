Overview

Your HbA1c monitoring at a glance: how well results agree with glucose, what needs attention, and how often IQC needs to run.

Data

Open an export with HbA1c and glucose results from the same samples. Files are read in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Distribution law

HbA1c quantiles follow a power law of glucose quantiles: ln Q(HbA1c) = α + β ln Q(glucose). The exponent β is close to 0.65 in every population studied, so only α needs local calibration.

Calibrate

Use 2–4 weeks of results from a period when IQC was in control. PartnerQC sets the centre and width of the HbA1c–glucose spread and an alarm limit for your daily volume.

Monitor

Every HbA1c result with glucose updates a moving average of the spread. The line crosses ±1 when HbA1c drifts away from what glucose predicts.

IQC methods

Compare the classic control-material techniques on the same results: Levey-Jennings with Westgard rules, EWMA and CUSUM. See which one reacts first to a shift, a drift or a loss of precision.

Simulate an error

Add an analyser error to the stream and compare how many unacceptable HbA1c results each QC strategy lets through before it detects the error.

Check a result

This is what the LIS or middleware does before releasing an HbA1c result: send the HbA1c and the glucose from the same sample, and act on the status.

QC plan

Estimate how often your laboratory needs to run HbA1c IQC when PartnerQC watches the results in between.

PartnerQC 0.1 is open-source software (MIT licence). It supports, and does not replace, the laboratory’s quality control procedures and professional judgement. Volumes in the QC plan are approximate and come from simulations in three Vietnamese hospitals and NHANES; validate them locally.
